# Quantum lab: first steps

On September 2025 I started studying Computer Science master's at VU/UvA in Amsterdam. My first aim was to get into the Quantum Computer Science program at UvA, since it seems to me that in computer science there's a bright future only for Machine Learning or Quantum Computing. Since I don't really enjoy dealing with data (I don't like the "non-determinism" of it), I chose the Quantum Computing side. I wrote my [bachelor thesis on VQE algorithm](virtualibiblioteka.vu.lt/permalink/370LABT_VU/4ffi2c/alma9913200291908452), even ran it on an actual IBM hardware. And I wanted to continue research in this field. I was late for the application and didn't get into the QuCS program - plan B was to take the basic theoretical CS, that I have more chances to get it, and then study twice as much as a normal master's student - both CS and QCS.

So the first thing I did - look at the QuCS program courses and chose the most interesting and important looking ones. One of them was Quantum Hardware. Sadly - as CS student I couldn't take it. But the great examination board made an exception for me (thank you very much!) and allowed to take the course, but with a condition that I would firstly pass QC, QIT and Intro to QH courses. Sadly, I passed only Intro to QH. But this year (2nd) I'm taking them again, should pass now.

Another thing I did - I tried to contact professors that are related to QC lab - how do I get to work in a lab? I got a really nice structured answer from the head of the [Strontium quantum gases group](https://www.strontiumbec.com/) (one of 2 quantum labs at UvA) dr. Florian Schreck. But yeah, it took me a year to actually sit down and look at the requirements listed, to get into the lab to write the master thesis on experimental topic (after 10 months of lab work) and after that more possibly getting into an experimental quantum computing PhD position. So let's overview what I should research about.

## Short summary

1. Know quantum mechanics well (maybe should read Griffiths)
2. Knowledge about atomic physics ([P2 course](https://coursecatalogue.uva.nl/en/courses/2026/1/58894) by Rene Gerritsma)
3. Knowledge about quantum optics ([P2 course](https://coursecatalogue.uva.nl/en/courses/2026/1/60699) by Robert Spreeuw)
4. (Master course level) knowledge about laser cooling and trapping (www.strontiumBEC.com -> Teaching)
5. Quantum gas generation and technology - very helpful (www.strontiumBEC.com -> Teaching)
6. Also quantum simulation course (need to get the slides)
7. Designing some electronics. But more important - optics

Then do a small lab project with bachelors, then write thesis for 10 months, then get into PhD.

So it looks like I could barely get into a lab. Maybe better try to get there with really small projects and pursue more the plan B with master thesis - doing everything more theoretically than experimentally.

## The plan

For each of the subjects make a post, cover a general summary and solved selected list of exercises. Then ask for lecture content from professors - repeat with the posts. Lastly, write an email listing out my progress and asking to make a mini project ein a lab together with bachelors. In parallel, figure out a list of topics that are possible to have with CS, QC and a little bit of experiment in a lab. Don't think I could actually do master thesis mostly experimental, so it's probably better to settle down to something I actually know. And for experimental QC PhD I would get experience with other small projects in the lab. Maybe also a bigger one to show when applying for PhD position. And another thing in parallel - try to get into a TCS research group. Finally, maybe combine something with algorithm complexity, since I will probably be TAing a course of this field.

Can I manage to take additional 2 courses, next to the normal program load? Maybe yes? If I don't manage, I can just fail them, I guess...
