## Dirac notation tricks

A few definitions before we begin:
- **projector** $P$ is a matrix, s.t. $P^2=P$, equivalently eigenvalues $\lambda \in \{0; 1\}$, equivalently $P = \sum_i \ketbra{\psi_i}{\psi_i}$;
- **unitary** $U$ is a matrix, s.t. $U^\dagger U = I$.

Now we can use dirac notation, for example, to prove no-cloning theorem. From unitary definition we can see that unitary preserves inner product between two states: $\bra{\psi}U^\dagger U \ket{\phi} = \braket{\psi}I{\phi} = \braket{\psi}{\phi}$. When proving no-cloning theorem we can just pick two different states and apply the same unitary, that is assumed to have the cloning property.

Let's briefly see via a few examples, how a simple projector works on a state.

$P \ket\psi = \ketbra{1}{1} (\frac{1}{2} \ket0 + \frac{\sqrt3}{2} \ket1) = \frac{1}{2} \ket1\braket{1}{0} + \frac{\sqrt3}{2} \ket1\braket{1}{1} = \frac{1}{2} \ket1 \cdot 0 + \frac{\sqrt3}{2} \ket1 \cdot 1 = \frac{\sqrt3}{2} \ket1$.

Here we see that in essence we just "took" the term from our state, that corresponds to the projector. However, that holds only for when the state is written in orthogonal basis. We can see that with an example:

$P \ket\psi = 5\ketbra{0}{0} (\frac{1}{2} \ket0 + \frac{\sqrt3}{2} \ket+) = 5\ketbra{0}{0} (\frac{1}{2} \ket0 + \frac{\sqrt3}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt2}(\ket0 + \ket1)) = 5\ketbra{0}{0} (\frac{\sqrt6}{2\sqrt2} \ket0 + \frac{\sqrt3}{2\sqrt2} \ket1) = 5 \cdot \frac{\sqrt6}{2\sqrt2} \ket0$.

And, of course, this can be extended linearly, so that you wouldn't need to expand the parenthesis every time, and just look where $\bra\psi$ corresponds to $\ket\psi$.

## Measurements and trace

Djordjevic mentions 3 types of measurements: generalized, POVM, projective. In BQC book there's also a mention of a weak measurement but we don't analyze it here. Also, for now we are only talking about 1 qubit systems, that is don't include partial traces in the measurements. However, we talk about both **pure states** and **mixed states** (convex combination of pure states, or to be more precise - PSD operators of trace 1).

For any type of quantum measurement we have:
1. a set of outcome symbols;
2. a set of measurement operators $\{M_i\}$, one for each outcome.

For projective measurements (that are supposedly the ones mentioned in QM postules) outcomes are selected from an **observable** by looking at it's eigenvalues. Observable examples: momentum $\hat p$, energy/hamiltonian $\hat H$, computational basis $Z$.

**Generalized measurement** is of the Kraus representation form (not discussed in this post) and is the most general way to represent a measurement. The only restriction for measurement operators is completeness $\sum_i M^\dagger M = I$ (probabilities sum to 1).
